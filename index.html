♦ LIVING AGRA ARTISAN CRAFT ♦

The Secrets of Parchin Kari (Pachekari)

When Emperor Shah Jahan commissioned the Taj Mahal in 1632, master lapidaries developed Parchin Kari (also known as Pietra Dura) — the art of hand-chiseled recesses into pure Makrana white marble, seamlessly inlaid with semi-precious gemstones.

In a single flower petal measuring less than an inch, artisans meticulously fit up to 35 individual stone facets of Lapis Lazuli (Persia), Malachite (Russia), Cornelian & Jasper (Rajasthan), and Mother-of-Pearl (Indian Ocean) with invisible tolerances.

Today in Agra, the direct descendants of the royal Mughal stone-carvers still keep this heritage alive. On our private tours, you can witness master craftsmen hand-shaping gems on traditional bow-lathes — with zero commercial purchase pressure.

THE LIVING PULSE OF AGRA

Beyond Monuments: The Soul of the City

Agra is a vibrant, sensory city with five centuries of culinary innovation and labyrinthine heritage bazaars.

Authentic Agra Petha & Dalmoth

No trip to Agra is complete without tasting the translucent candied white ash gourd sweet (Petha) perfected by Mughal royal chefs, paired with spicy crunchy Dalmoth lentil namkeen at centuries-old local confectioners.

Kinari Bazaar & Old City Spice Lanes

Walk through narrow cobblestone alleys behind the Jama Masjid where generations of silversmiths, bridal Zardozi embroidery masters, and incense purveyors preserve the authentic Mughal bazaar atmosphere.

Bedai-Jalebi Breakfast Culture

Experience the local morning ritual: crispy puffed, urad-dal-stuffed Bedai puris served with spicy fenugreek potato curry, followed by hot, dripping sweet saffron jalebis.

Is the Taj Mahal open every day of the week? +
The Taj Mahal opens 30 minutes before sunrise and closes 30 minutes before sunset every day EXCEPT FRIDAYS. On Fridays, the monument is strictly closed for public visitors. However, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Itimad-ud-Daulah, and Mehtab Bagh remain open on Fridays, allowing for alternative full-day itineraries.
What are the official entry ticket fees in 2026? +
Official Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) rates: Foreign Tourists pay ₹1,100 standard entry + ₹200 for main mausoleum dome access (total ₹1,300 / approx $16 USD). Indian citizens pay ₹50 + ₹200 dome access (total ₹250). Children under 15 enter free. All TajMahal.City private tours include fast-track advance online ticket procurement so you bypass queue ticket windows completely.
What items are strictly prohibited inside the monument? +
Strict airport-style security scans operate at all gates. Prohibited items include: tripods, camera gimbals/stabilizers, drones, electronic chargers/cables, knives, lighters, cigarettes, and chewing gum. Safe cloakrooms/lockers are available at both East and West gates to securely store unauthorized items before entry.
